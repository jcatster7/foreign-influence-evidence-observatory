Source: X's [moderation-research archive page](https://transparency.x.com/pt/reports/moderation-research) and [information-operations archive page](https://transparency.x.com/pt/reports/information-operations), inspected 2026-09-20 UTC in Portuguese because the English page did not render through the web reader. Site HTML can change between requests, so the entry hashes and dated source URL matter more than a live-page byte match.

The metadata index records **48 labeled archive entries** visible under 12 release-month sections, each with its printed account count and source position. X separately states that it publicly shared **37 datasets** of state-linked manipulation campaigns between October 2018 and early 2022, then stopped new public releases when it launched its Moderation Research Consortium. The 48 listed entry labels and X's 37-dataset historical statement are different source-level counts. Their relationship has not been reconciled; neither is an independent-campaign count or a denominator for a meta-analysis. Account counts printed for different entries must not be summed as unique people or operators.

X says the downloadable archive contains profile information, posts, and media for accounts it attributed to government-linked information operations. Its public archive hashes selected identifiers for accounts with fewer than 5,000 followers. Deleted posts and media are absent; some attributed accounts never posted actively, so the represented account corpus can be smaller than the enforced-account count. X describes taking precautions against false positives and provides an appeal route, which means inclusion is a platform enforcement label rather than independently verified ground truth for every account. The page does not provide observed feed delivery, a ranking counterfactual, or audience outcomes.

The archive page asks for an email submission and agreement to X's Developer Agreement and Policy before downloads. This intake uses **only public page metadata**. It did not submit an email, accept terms, retrieve account/post/media files, or access the restricted un-hashed consortium archive. Any later archive use requires a rights review and a separate record of what was actually acquired; linked metadata alone cannot supply account-level O or A labels.
